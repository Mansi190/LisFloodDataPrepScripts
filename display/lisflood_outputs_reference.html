LISFLOOD — Hydrological Rainfall-Runoff Model

LISFLOOD is a spatially distributed, physically-based rainfall-runoff model developed by the Floods group of the Natural Hazards Project, Joint Research Centre (JRC), European Commission. It simulates the full water balance of a catchment on a regular grid at daily (or sub-daily) time steps, and is designed for large and trans-national river basins with grid resolutions from 100 m (medium catchments) to 0.1° (~10 km, continental scale).

Warm start / Cold start: The output of a "water balance run" (cold start) can be used to provide the initial conditions of a "flood run" (warm start) — enabling realistic antecedent soil moisture and groundwater levels before simulating individual flood events.

Simulated Processes & Modules

Snow & Frost Index

When the average daily temperature falls below 1°C, all precipitation is treated as snow and accumulates on the soil surface until it melts. Snowmelt uses a degree-day factor method extended by Speers et al., with rainfall-enhanced melt and a seasonally varying melt coefficient CSeasonal:

M = Cm · CSeasonal · (1 + 0.01·R·Δt)(Tavg − Tm) · Δt

Snow melt and accumulation are modelled separately for 3 sub-pixel elevation zones to capture orographic effects. A low value of Cm indicates slow snowmelt; CSeasonal peaks in June and is lowest in December.

A frost index F tracks cumulative frozen conditions. When F > 56, all soil processes (transpiration, evaporation, infiltration, drainage to groundwater) are set to zero — any rainfall bypasses the soil and becomes direct surface runoff until F drops back below 56.

Sub-grid Land Cover — HRU Approach

Rather than assigning a single dominant land cover per pixel, LISFLOOD models four aggregated land cover classes as fractions within each pixel:

The spatial distribution and frequency of each class is defined as a percentage of the whole represented area of the pixel — capturing sub-grid variability lost in the previous dominant-value approach.

Soil and groundwater processes — evaporation, transpiration, infiltration, preferential flow, soil moisture redistribution, and groundwater flow — are valid only for the forest and other fractions.

Interception of Rainfall

Rainfall intercepted by vegetation canopy is stored and later evaporated or drips to the soil as leaf drainage. Uses a storage-based equation (Aston, 1978; Merriam, 1960):

Int = Smax · [1 − exp(−k · R · Δt / Smax)]

Smax [mm] is the maximum interception capacity derived from the Leaf Area Index (LAI); k accounts for vegetation density. Leaf drainage Dint empties the interception store with a 1-day time constant.

Evaporation & Transpiration

Transpiration (Ta) is limited by a water stress factor RWS that decreases linearly to zero as soil moisture falls from the critical level (wcrit) to wilting point (wwp). Energy used for interception evaporation is subtracted from available energy for transpiration.

Direct soil evaporation (ESa) decreases as topsoil dries using a √Dslr relationship, where Dslr = days since last significant rain event. Both processes are set to zero when the frost index exceeds its threshold.

Total pixel ET is a weighted sum across all four land cover fractions:
ETpixel = fforest·ETforest + fdr·Edr + fwater·Ewater + (1−fforest−fdr−fwater)·ETother

Infiltration & Surface Runoff

In the permeable fraction of each pixel (1−fdr), water available for infiltration per time step is:

Wav = R·Δt + M + Dint − Int

Infiltration capacity is estimated using the Xinanjiang (VIC/ARNO) model. The saturated fraction As is related to total soil moisture through an empirical shape parameter b — higher b means more water bypasses the soil directly to groundwater (sieve-like behaviour). The remaining excess water becomes surface runoff Rs, routed overland to the nearest channel by the kinematic wave equations.

Preferential Bypass Flow

A fraction of the water available for infiltration bypasses the soil matrix and drains directly to the upper groundwater zone — representing macro-pore flow and fast preferential pathways:

Dpref,gw = Wav · (w1 / ws1)cpref

This term becomes increasingly important as the soil approaches saturation. The exponent cpref is a calibration parameter; only the permeable fraction of each pixel contributes to preferential flow.

Soil Moisture Redistribution

Moisture flux through the two-layer soil profile — D1,2 (topsoil → subsoil) and D2,gw (subsoil → upper groundwater zone) — is driven entirely by gravity (zero matric potential gradient assumed). Hydraulic conductivity K(θ) follows the Van Genuchten equation:

K(w) = Ks · (relative saturation)½ · [1 − (1 − relative sat1/m)m]²

A Courant-number sub-stepping procedure ensures mass balance at daily time steps. If the soil is frozen (F > threshold), both D1,2 and D2,gw are set to zero.

Groundwater — Two-Zone Linear Reservoir Model

Groundwater storage and transport are modelled using two parallel linear reservoirs (similar to the HBV-96 model):

Upper Zone (UZ) — fast groundwater + subsurface flow through macro-pores. Outflow:
Quz = (1/Tuz) · UZ · Δt

Lower Zone (LZ) — slow groundwater component generating base flow. Outflow:
Qlz = (1/Tlz) · LZ · Δt

A fixed percolation flux Duz,lz = min(GWperc·Δt, UZ) transfers water from UZ to LZ each time step. An optional loss term Dloss from LZ represents deep percolation to systems that do not follow catchment boundaries (e.g. deep aquifers, extraction wells) — use with care; start at zero. Both Tuz and Tlz are obtained by calibration.

All groundwater outflow (Quz + Qlz) is instantaneously routed to the nearest downstream channel pixel. Channel flow is then routed through the network using the kinematic wave equations.

Soil Surface State — Days Since Last Rain

1 variable
number of days since last rain
dslr.nc  /  dslf.nc  /  dsli.nc
days
▼ expand
What is it?
Counts consecutive days without significant rainfall (threshold: SoilMoistureThreshold parameter, typically >1 mm/day). Controls the formation of a soil surface crust: longer dry spells → denser crust → reduced infiltration capacity on the next storm. Counter resets to zero the day after rain exceeds the threshold. Three files: dslr.nc (Other), dslf.nc (Forest), dsli.nc (Irrigated). Because forest intercepts much more rainfall, a given event may not reset the forest counter if throughfall is below the threshold — forest can accumulate higher values during moderate rainfall periods.
Chain: dslr / dslF → soil crust factor → reduces effective Ks1 → less infiltration → more surface runoff on first storm

What is it?
Fast-response (upper) groundwater storage. Two files: uz.nc for the Other land cover fraction and uzf.nc for the Forest fraction. storage in lower groundwater zone
lz.nc
mm
▼ expand
What is it?
Slow-response (lower) groundwater storage. Unlike the upper zone, LISFLOOD keeps a single shared lower zone per pixel (no forest/irrigated variants) — one file, lz.nc. Represents deep soil water and the shallow unconfined aquifer. Receives water from the upper zone (percUZLZ) and loses it via baseflow to the channel (qLz — slow, sustained) and deep percolation loss to bedrock (GwLoss).

6 variables
Reference ET (basin average)
etUps.tss
mm/day
▼ expand
What is it?
Potential evapotranspiration computed by LISVAP (the pre-processor) using the Penman-Monteith equation for a reference crop (well-watered short grass). This is the atmospheric demand — how much water the atmosphere would evaporate if water were unlimited. Spatially averaged over the upstream basin.

LISFLOOD multiplies this by crop coefficients (Kc, per land cover type) and reduces it by a soil moisture stress factor to get actual ET. The difference between ETref and actual ET reflects water stress.
ETref (input, from LISVAP) × Kc → Potential ET per fraction × Stress factor → Actual ET

5 variables

What is it?
Water currently stored on the canopy (leaves and stems) for each land cover fraction. Filled by rainfall; emptied by evaporation (intercepted water has priority over soil evaporation) and leaf drainage once the canopy is full.

What is it?

Mass Balance Error & Numerical Diagnostics
mbError.tss  /  mbErrorMm.tss  /  NoSubStepsChannel.tss  /  steps.tss
m³ / mm / count
DefaultAlways written
▼ expand
What are these?
Four diagnostic outputs documented as default LISFLOOD outputs (note: the current v4.3.1 runs did not write the mbError files — recent versions may gate them behind repMBTs):

• mbError.tss: cumulative water balance error (m³) — should be small relative to total basin storage; large values indicate a numerical or input problem.
• mbErrorMm.tss: same error expressed as basin-average mm.
• NoSubStepsChannel.tss: number of kinematic-wave routing sub-steps per day — the model automatically subdivides the timestep to maintain numerical stability; high values (e.g. >10) indicate a steep channel or rapidly rising hydrograph.
• steps.tss: Courant-number controlled sub-steps for the soil moisture routine.

These files are your first diagnostic check — if mbError grows, something is wrong with your water balance.
Healthy run: mbError should be <1% of annual runoff volume and should not trend upward over time.
Always written — no flag needed.
